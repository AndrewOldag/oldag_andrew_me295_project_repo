## Project proposal

### Research question
- Can an LLM agent, guided by deterministic beam-kinematics checks, decide when a strain-rate measurement of a growing root can be repaired and when it must be declined, more reliably than a fixed script, a rule-based remedy tree and a single agent without the checks?
- Setting: a growing root is a slender rod that generates its own strain. Its axial strain rate sets elongation; the difference across the section sets the rate of bending. The field is measured by image correlation on time-lapse movies, and a measurement can fail silently (drift, displacement per frame above the patch size, midline jitter, low texture, width change)
- The paper makes claims about agent behaviour only. Kinematics only: no stress, stiffness or constitutive law

### Intended users
- Groups running high-throughput growth kinematics: root or hypocotyl strain-rate fields from many time-lapse movies
- Today each profile is checked by eye and unreliable ones are removed by hand (Youssef et al. 2018; Platre et al. 2024 report 8-25 % of roots lost). The system gives each measurement accept, repair or decline with a stated reason, so manual review is limited to flagged cases

### Why an agent
- A fixed script or an optimizer always returns an answer. The claim tested: an agent can name the fault, choose a structurally different remedy, and decline an untrustworthy result
- The decision is made against deterministic checks outside the model, not the model's own confidence
- Tested on five held-out faults never used in development

### Agent systems for scientific workflows
- Wei et al. 2025, From AI for Science to Agentic Science (arXiv:2508.14111). Four autonomy levels; provenance of tool versions and parameters called required; hallucination can be amplified between agents. Here: a level 2 system; each run records the freeze tag and commit
- Yao et al. 2023, ReAct (ICLR 2023; arXiv:2210.03629). Reason-act-observe loop over three typed calls. Reasoning errors, including repetitive loops, 47 % of ReAct failures (Table 2). Here: the same loop over a fixed action space; the single agent hit the step limit on 13 of 25 development runs
- Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017, Selective classification (NeurIPS 2017; arXiv:1705.08500). Reject option; selective risk vs coverage. Here: decline is the reject option, false accepts the selective risk; unnecessary declines are scored so that declining everything does not score well

### Setting: root kinematics tools
- KymoRod (Bastien et al. 2016), PatchTrack (Henry et al. 2023), Chavarría-Krauser et al. 2008, Krupař et al. 2026: each returns a velocity or strain-rate profile; trust in the profile is decided by a person after the run
- Quality control is manual: Youssef et al. 2018 checked profiles by eye; Platre et al. 2024 report roots lost but no automatic test of a measurement
- The checks come from kinematic relations in the same literature: flank REGR difference tied to the material curvature rate for a rod of constant width (Silk & Erickson 1978; Chavarría-Krauser 2006; Bastien et al. 2014)

### Synthesis
- The tracker stays fixed. This project adds the layer around it: deterministic checks that any correct rod measurement satisfies, an agent that names the fault and repairs or declines, and scoring with known truth
- New against these papers: whether a result can be trusted is decided by beam kinematics, not by the model's confidence
